Study and identification of various types of flames generated in oxyacetylene gas welding.

Procedure

This simulation demonstrates the formation of different Oxy-Acetylene flames (carburising/reducing, neutral, and oxidising) by adjusting the acetylene and oxygen supply.

  1. Open Acetylene Valve:
    • Slightly open the acetylene valve on the torch.
  2. Observe Orange Flame:
    • The flame appears yellowish-orange due to incomplete combustion.
    • This is an unstable flame with excess acetylene.
  3. Open Oxygen Valve (Low Flow):
    • Slowly open the oxygen valve and keep the flow low.
    • Oxygen begins to mix with acetylene for partial combustion.
  4. Observe Reducing Flame:
    • The flame becomes longer with a feathery edge.
    • This carburising/reducing flame has excess acetylene.
  5. Increase Oxygen Flow to Balance Gases:
    • Gradually increase oxygen until both gases are balanced.
    • A well-defined bright inner cone becomes visible.
  6. Observe Neutral Flame:
    • The neutral flame has a sharp inner cone with no feathering.
    • This flame is ideal for welding mild steel and general-purpose use.
  7. Further Increase Oxygen Flow:
    • Increase oxygen flow beyond balance condition.
    • The flame cone becomes shorter, sharper, and produces a hissing sound.
  8. Observe Oxidising Flame:
    • The oxidising flame has excess oxygen.
    • It produces higher temperature but may cause oxidation of steel.
