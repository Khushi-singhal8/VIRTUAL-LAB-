To study butt joint welding using gas oxy-acetylene method

Theory

Theory

Oxy-acetylene welding is a fusion welding process that burns acetylene in oxygen to produce a very high-temperature flame used to melt and join metals. The chemical combustion of acetylene in oxygen releases large heat and the reaction may be written as:

2C2H2 + 5O2 → 4CO2 + 2H2O + (high Heat)

Under correct mixing the flame reaches temperatures sufficient to melt common structural metals; this makes oxy-acetylene useful for welding, brazing and flame cutting.Typical flame types are neutral, oxidizing, and carburizing (reducing) — each obtained by altering the oxygen/fuel ratio and each having different effects on base metal chemistry and weld quality.

A butt joint is formed when the edges of two plates lie in the same plane and are joined along their touching edges. Good joint preparation (cleaning, correct bevel/edge angle, correct root gap and tack welding) plus flame control are essential to produce full penetration, strong, defect-free welds. Microstructure and mechanical properties of gaswelded butt joints have been investigated in literature and show that joint quality depends strongly on heat input, joint geometry and post-weld handling.

Applications:

  • Joining mild steel plates in fabrication workshops.
  • Repair of automotive body panels and frames.
  • Construction of small pipelines and thin-wall tubing.
  • Sheet metal work in HVAC and appliance manufacturing.
  • Educational training for welding fundamentals.

Advantages:

  • Simple equipment with low initial investment cost.
  • Suitable for a wide range of metals and thicknesses (especially thin plates).
  • No electrical power source needed — portable for field work.
  • Allows both welding and cutting with the same torch (by changing tips).
  • Smooth, clean welds possible with skilled operator control.

Disadvantages:

  • Slower process compared to electric arc welding for thick sections.
  • Greater heat-affected zone (HAZ) — can lead to distortion.
  • Not economical for high-volume or large-thickness welding.
  • Requires continuous gas supply (oxygen and acetylene cylinders).
  • Safety hazards from open flame, high-pressure gases, and fumes.

Apparatus

    Protective equipment

    • Exhaust/ventilation fan — keeps fumes away from breathing zone. (OSHA:ventilation & safe storage guidance.)
    • Eye shield / goggles — protect from intense UV/IR and sparks
    • Leather gloves and apron — protect hands and body from heat and sparks.

    Operating equipment

    • Oxygen Cylinder— high-pressure steel cylinder with regulator (black coding in many standards). Use only oil-free fittings.
    • Acetylene cylinders — fuel gas cylinder (acetylene normally dissolved in acetone in cylinder).
    • Pressure regulators — mounted on each cylinder to control delivery pressure.
    • Hoses — flexible, color-coded (commonly black for oxygen, red for acetylene).Never interchange.
    • Torch (Welding handle + tips/nozzles) — mixes gases and directs the flame.Different tip sizes for different thicknesses.
    • Striker — flint striker for safe ignition (do not use matches/lighters).
    • Filler roads matching filler metal (if required) for butt joint (e.g., ER70S-6 for mild steel).
    • Clamps / workbench — rigid fixing to prevent movement and ensure the joint gap stays constant.
    • Chipping hammer & wire brush — for slag removal/cleaning after welding.

Miscellaneous:file, beveling tool (grinder), measuring scale, soap solution for leak checks.

Precautions:

  • Keep work area clean and free from flammable materials.
  • Use flashback arrestors and check valves.
  • Keep cylinder valves closed when not in use.
  • Purge hoses before lighting to clear trapped gases.
