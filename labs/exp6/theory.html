Study and identification of various types of flames generated in oxyacetylene gas welding.

Theory

The welding is a process of joining two similar or dissimilar metals by fusion, with or without the application of pressure and with or without the use of filler metal. The fusion of metal takes place by means of heat. The heat may be obtained from blacksmith's fire, electric arc, resistance or by chemical reaction.

Fig (a):welding operation

The welding is extensively used in fabrication as an alternative method for casting and forging and as a replacement for bolted and riveted joints. It is also used as a repair medium e.g., to reunite metal at a crank, to build up a small part that has broken off such as gear tooth or to repair a worn surface such as bearing surface.

Applications of Welding:

  • Automobile construction
  • Rail road equipment
  • Ships.
  • Aircraft operation.
  • Bridges construction.
  • Storage Tanks.
  • Piping and pipelines
  • Household furniture
  • Repair of broken and damaged parts etc.

Oxyacetylene gas welding is one of the most common types of gas welding techniques, using a combination of oxygen and acetylene gas to produce a flame hot enough to melt and fuse metals. The welding torch allows precise control of gas flow, creating flames of different characteristics.

Advantages of oxy acetylene welding :

  1. It is easy to learn.
  2. OA equipment can also be used to "flame cut" large pieces of material.
  3. Total welding cost is less.
  4. Wide varieties of electrodes are available.
  5. The welding equipment is simple, less costly and portable.
  6. Welding can be carried out in any position with the highest weld qualities

Disadvantages of oxy acetylene welding :

  1. OA weld lines are much rougher in appearance than other kinds of welds, and require more finishing if neatness is required.
  2. OA welds have large heat affected zones (areas around the weld line that have had their mechanical properties adversely affected by the welding process).
  3. Welding control is difficult as compare to MIG welding.
  4. Because of flux coating chances of slag entrapment are more.
Fig (b): Oxt-Acetylene Gas

Understanding the types of flames generated and their applications is essential for safe and effective welding. There are three primary types of oxyacetylene flames based on the ratio of oxygen to acetylene:

  • Neutral Flame: This flame has a well-defined inner luminous cone and an outer envelope. It has a temperature around 3300°C and does not oxidize or carburize the metal. It is ideal for welding steels and cast iron.

    Fig (c): Neutral Flame

  • Carburizing Flame: This flame has a longer, brighter inner cone and a feathery middle cone. It adds carbon to the metal and is suitable for welding high-carbon steels, lead, and aluminum where oxidation must be avoided.

    Fig (d): Carburizing Flame

  • Oxidizing Flame: This flame has a shorter, sharp inner cone and a loud hissing sound. It is hotter than the neutral flame and is used for cutting and welding metals like brass or bronze that require oxidation.

    Fig (e): Oxidizing flame

The flame type significantly affects the metallurgical properties of the weld, such as hardness, strength, and brittleness. A neutral flame is the most commonly used for welding, while the oxidizing flame is used mainly in cutting operations.

Safe handling of the gas cylinders, checking for leaks, and setting correct pressure values are essential parts of this operation. This experiment helps learners visually identify and understand the behaviour of each flame type through simulation, without the risks associated with actual flame handling.
